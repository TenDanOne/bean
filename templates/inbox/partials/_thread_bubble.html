{% load humanize %} {% comment %} One turn of the conversation. The only bubble in the inbox. Takes `turn`, a dict built by _thread_turns in views.py — side, author, body, timestamp. The deciding happens there on purpose: this used to be three partials, each working out "who is this from" its own way, and they drifted into three visual languages. The worst of it was invisible — a reply you had just sent was drawn by one template and, once sync confirmed it, by another, so the same message changed shape underneath you. A direct message and a comment thread render through this identically. For whoever is answering they are the same act: someone wrote, we answer, maybe they continue. What differs between them is where the message came from, and that is a line of metadata, not a different way of reading a screen. The avatar is always there, on every side and every kind. In a publication thread several people talk at once and a face is how you follow who is who without re-reading names; in a DM there is only one of them and it looks redundant — but keeping it is what makes the two surfaces read the same, and that consistency is worth more than the pixels. {% endcomment %}
